What a conflict is, and what to check

A device sent a copy of a session file whose bytes neither continue the copy the lake holds nor are continued by it. The lake kept both copies. It merged nothing, lost nothing, and the session still shows the head it had.

Most conflicts need nothing from you. A few are worth a look:

  • The copy and the head came from different machines. Two machines wrote the same session: a shared home directory, or a session carried between machines and resumed.
  • The copy is shorter than the head. The harness rewrote or truncated the file, for example after compacting a conversation.
  • Several copies at one path. The harness rewrote the file and kept appending to it. Each post is another copy, and the session in the lake has stopped moving.

Raw bytes of both sides are on each session's Raw tab, for an admin. A resolved conflict leaves this list and its bytes stay.

What these numbers are

  • An update is a change of a session's head that the lake accepted: a new session, a transcript that grew, or a snapshot export rewritten. A repeated upload, a stale upload and a divergent copy are not updates.
  • Net logical change adds up new minus old head size. It is not network traffic and not disk growth, and it is negative when rewrites shrank sessions.
  • Hatched buckets were not measured: they ended before recording began. They are not zero.
  • Purging a session removes its updates from past buckets. Restoring a backup rewinds this history to the backup.

What these numbers are

  • Disk use is the blocks the lake directory's files and directories occupy, as du counts them, sampled when serve starts and then hourly. It is not the size of what agents uploaded.
  • Compression against raw is the size of the current version of every uploaded file, which is what the machines hold, divided by the disk use of the stored blobs. The blobs also keep every earlier version, so that history counts against the ratio; a version that grew costs only a record, because it shares its bytes with the next. Duplicate files are current files whose bytes match another's exactly; the lake stores them once. Continuations of a session are not duplicates.
  • Stored blobs, the catalog and the audit log are the lake's record. Normalized events, parquet and the search index are derived from them and can be rebuilt.
  • Last contact is the last request a device's token made since serve started; a restart clears it. Last new data is the newest upload the lake had not seen before. A machine can be in contact with nothing new to send.
  • Freshness is active within a day of the later of the two, idle within a week, quiet after that.
